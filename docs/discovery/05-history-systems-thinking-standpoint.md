# History (systems-thinking standpoint) — Discovery Source List

Status: needs-human by reviewer loop (iteration 2). 8 subareas, 14 sources, 5 queries. Generated 2026-08-03.

## Subareas
### 1. Innovation history / Age of Invention

Full: independent sources parallel to (but not duplicating) the subscribed Age of Invention newsletter; invention, productivity and progress with quantitative grounding.

- **Roots of Progress** (`rootsofprogress.org`) — independent blog — high · crawl: https://rootsofprogress.org/feed
  Jason Crawford's series on the history of technology adoption and the drivers of progress — direct systems/constraints angle on invention history.
- **The Puzzle and Its Pieces** (`thepuzzleanditspieces.substack.com`) — newsletter — high · crawl: https://thepuzzleanditspieces.substack.com/feed
  PEG's series on economic progress, technology adoption and invention ('What Happens in the Gap') — systems angle on why progress happens, complementing Age of Invention without duplicating it.

### 2. Quantitative / cliometric history

Full: long-run measurement, cycles of history proxied statistically, and historical datasets; the most explicitly 'systems' subarea.

- **Peter Turchin's Substack (Cliodynamics)** (`peterturchin.substack.com`) — newsletter / academic-adjacent — high · crawl: https://peterturchin.substack.com/feed
  Quantitative history of empires, cycles and collective action — the canonical cliodynamics voice applying systems theory to history.
- **NBER — Development of the American Economy & Macrohistory** (`nber.org`) — data / registry + research — high · crawl: https://back.nber.org/rss/aginghealth.xml
  Registry of historical macro series (prices, output, factor inputs) and working papers on US long-run economic history — structured data for quantitative history.
- **Economic Growth in History** (`nofuturepast.wordpress.com`) — independent blog — high · crawl: https://nofuturepast.wordpress.com/feed
  Quantitative economic history on long-run GDP reconstruction, Maddison-dataset discrepancies and growth measurement — cliometric/measurement angle replacing the feed-less Cliodynamics journal.

### 3. Logistical & military history (systems view)

Implied but core to user's 'logistics' interest; ACOUP is the strongest single source on how supply chains and physical constraints shaped military and political outcomes.

- **Edge Induced Cohesion** (`edgeinducedcohesion.blog`) — independent blog — high · crawl: https://edgeinducedcohesion.blog/feed
  "A Historian's Eye View of the World" — logistics, feeding armies and material constraints behind campaigns (e.g. white paper on Sherman's Georgia logistics); the strongest ACOUP-parallel for systems military history.

### 4. Geography's role in history

Full: how terrain, water, climate and resource endowments shaped development paths — a central stated interest.

- **From Poverty to Progress** (`frompovertytoprogress.substack.com`) — newsletter — high · crawl: https://frompovertytoprogress.substack.com/feed
  Stefen Klein's multi-series economic history covering 'why some nations became rich' with explicit geography, soils and tsetse-fly/cattle crop-yield causal analysis.
- **Sam Matey-Coste (China's Hydrological History)** (`sammatey.substack.com`) — newsletter — high · crawl: https://sammatey.substack.com/feed
  Geography-driven history of water systems, flood control and canal infrastructure shaping China's state and economy — physical-constraints history.

### 5. History of economics & economic thought

Full: the intellectual and measurement history of economics itself, plus long-run economic history research.

- **Davis Kedrosky — Economic History journal blog** (`blog.daviskedrosky.com`) — independent blog / academic commentary — high · crawl: https://blog.daviskedrosky.com/feed
  Series on 'The History of Economic History' tracing how fields and their quantitative methods evolved — meta-history of economics.

### 6. History of politics, institutions & political economy

Full: how institutions, incentives and market structure (systems) shaped political-economic outcomes over time.

- **The Diff** (`thediff.co`) — newsletter / independent analysis — high · crawl: https://thediff.co/feed
  Byrne Hobart's systems take on how financial and industrial structure evolved historically — institutions and incentives applied to market history.
- **Free Markets & Firepower** (`freemarketsandfirepower.substack.com`) — newsletter — high · crawl: https://freemarketsandfirepower.substack.com/feed
  Economic and political history linking fiscal/monetary systems ('price of time') to state power — history of politics and economics through incentives.
- **Marginal Revolution** (`marginalrevolution.com`) — independent blog — high · crawl: https://marginalrevolution.com/feed
  Market/economic-history commentary with heavy systems, incentives and constraining-geography content; complements the Diff for political economy.

### 7. Long-run data, technology adoption & material constraints

Full: empirical long-run series on growth, energy, technology diffusion and physical limits.

- **Our World in Data — Technology & Growth long-run series** (`ourworldindata.org`) — data / registry + analysis — high · crawl: https://ourworldindata.org/feed
  Registry of long-run historical series (technology adoption, energy, GDP per capita) with systems-oriented writeups — complements NBER and OWID's own feeds.

### 8. Geography / systems-resilience (broad context)

Partial/implied — one cross-cutting source bridging geography, shipping chokepoints and system fragility.

- **The Great Simplification (systems-history episodes)** (`thegreatsimplification.com`) — podcast — high · crawl: https://thegreatsimplification.com/feed
  Systems economist Nate Hagens interviews historians on chokepoints, shipping and material constraints shaping civilization — geography-systems angle.
